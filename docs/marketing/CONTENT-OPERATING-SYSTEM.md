## 1. The two engines

Two related but distinct engines. They share technical credibility; they never share
language, CTA, channel selection, proof, distribution, or landing pages.

| | **Engine A — Residential Demand** | **Engine B — Commercial / TAB Authority** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | Homeowners (repair, comfort/airflow, maintenance, replacement) | Mechanical contractors, GCs, property/facility managers, engineers, gyms, retail, churches, restaurants, offices |
| Channels | Google Search, GBP, YouTube, Facebook, Nextdoor, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, email, SMS (appropriate use) | LinkedIn, YouTube, direct email, direct business outreach, website, Google, selected Facebook/GBP support |
| Landing pages | Repair & Diagnostics; Airflow & Ductwork; Maintenance; Replacement & Installation | Commercial Service & Maintenance; TAB & Commissioning Support |
| Proof | Symptom → measured finding → plain-language explanation | Traverse data, VAV/RTU readings, outside-air verification, deficiency reports, closeout documentation |
| CTA | Call/request diagnostics; schedule maintenance; free estimate | Discuss TAB/project scope; commercial service discussion |

**Rule: do not make every content item serve both audiences.** When a subject is genuinely
relevant to both (e.g. static pressure, ductwork), produce a separate commercial adaptation
with commercial proof and CTA — do not reword the residential post.

## 3. Funnel architecture

Six conversion paths: Repair & Diagnostics · Airflow & Ductwork · Maintenance · Replacement &
Installation · Commercial HVAC · TAB / Commissioning Support.

```
CONTENT / SEARCH
  ↓
RELEVANT OWNING PAGE
  ↓
ONE PRIMARY CTA
  ↓
LEAD RECORD
  ↓
FAST FOLLOW-UP
  ↓
BOOKED SERVICE / ESTIMATE / PROJECT DISCUSSION
  ↓
FIELD EXECUTION
  ↓
FIELD PROOF
  ↓
REVIEW / REFERRAL REQUEST
  ↓
REACTIVATION / REPEAT BUSINESS
```

**Do not route every campaign to the homepage.** Hot-room content → Airflow & Ductwork.
No-cool content → Repair & Diagnostics. Maintenance content → Maintenance. Quote-comparison
content → Replacement & Installation. TAB content → TAB & Commissioning Support. Commercial
content → Commercial Service.

## 6. Weekly production system

A realistic lean-crew workflow. Total owner time: roughly 3–4 hours across the week plus
field capture that happens during normal work.

### MONDAY — PLAN (30–45 minutes)

- Confirm the weekly anchor topic (from the 12-week map / calendar).
- Confirm target audience and owning page.
- Confirm **one** CTA.
- Write five hooks.
- Prepare the shot list.
- Identify any legitimate Field Proof opportunity from scheduled field work.
- Verify privacy/recording permission when needed.

### TUESDAY — FILM ANCHOR

Film one 4–8 minute anchor. Default structure:

1. Problem / hook
2. What the customer notices
3. Possible causes
4. What SCS actually checks
5. Real tools / measurements
6. Common misconception
7. Decision / correction / next step
8. CTA

**Avoid generic intros** ("Hey guys, Aaron here with Sunshine Climate Solutions…"). Prefer
problem-first hooks, e.g.: *"If your AC runs all day but one bedroom still stays hot, the AC
itself might not be the actual problem."*

### WEDNESDAY — SHORT-FORM BATCH

Create 3–4 clips from the anchor/field footage:

- **Clip A:** strongest problem hook.
- **Clip B:** myth / misconception.
- **Clip C:** tool or measurement.
- **Clip D:** Field Proof.

Do **not** manufacture 12 weak clips simply to hit a quantity target.

### THURSDAY — DISTRIBUTION

Adapt and publish: YouTube anchor · YouTube Short · Instagram Reel · TikTok · Facebook ·
Nextdoor · GBP · LinkedIn where relevant.

The same subject may be reused, but **do not paste the exact same caption everywhere** —
each platform receives native framing.

### FRIDAY — REVENUE + OPTIMIZATION (45–60 minutes)

This is marketing work, not optional admin:

- Follow up unclosed estimates.
- Request legitimate reviews (see §10).
- Add useful job photos to GBP.
- Add legitimate Field Proof to the website where a slot exists.
- Review new leads by source.
- Update the attribution sheet (see §12).
- Contact commercial prospects (see §14).
- Record weekly KPIs in `WEEKLY-MARKETING-SCORECARD.md`.
- Decide one improvement for next week.

## 8. Field Proof SOP

Field Proof is a permanent recurring series. **Never staged. Never fabricated.** Every Field
Proof asset contains four elements:

1. **CONTEXT** — what was the complaint or project condition?
   *(Example: "3.5-ton system with weak airflow complaints.")*
2. **MEASUREMENT / OBSERVATION** — what was actually measured or observed? (Static pressure,
   temperature, airflow, duct condition, electrical measurement, outside-air measurement,
   equipment fault.)
3. **INTERPRETATION** — what the measurement means **for this system**. Never present one
   number as a universal threshold unless properly qualified.
4. **OUTCOME / NEXT ACTION** — what was corrected, recommended, verified, or still requires
   action.

### Field capture checklist (when appropriate)

- Wide equipment shot
- Clean equipment photo
- Relevant data plate when privacy/safety permits
- Instrument reading
- Duct / problem area
- Before condition
- After condition
- 10–30 second technician explanation
- Finished workmanship
- Vertical video

### Do NOT capture

Customer addresses, paperwork, phone numbers, faces, license plates, or personal data —
unless appropriate permission exists.

### Caption formula

**Problem → Measurement → Meaning → Action → CTA**

*EXAMPLE (illustrative, not a real measurement):*

> "This system was cooling, but airflow remained poor. Total external static was elevated,
> with most of the restriction on the return side. We verified the restriction before
> recommending changes instead of simply increasing blower speed. If airflow feels weak or one
> room stays hotter than the rest, we can measure the system instead of guessing."

Do not fabricate measurements for documentation examples; use clearly labeled EXAMPLE
placeholders.

## 10. Review system

Full workflow: `docs/marketing/REVIEW-GROWTH-SYSTEM.md`. Summary:

**Ask after:** successful repair · completed maintenance · successful replacement · resolved
comfort complaint · appropriate commercial completion.

**Process:**

1. Confirm the customer is satisfied.
2. Send the direct review link approximately **30–60 minutes** after completion.
3. Send **one** polite reminder after approximately **2–3 days** if necessary.
4. Stop afterward.

**Never:** gate reviews, require 5-star ratings, fabricate reviews, or offer improper
incentives for positive reviews.

## 11. Referrals

After legitimate positive feedback (review received, thank-you message, repeat compliment),
include the referral prompt:

> "If you know anyone nearby who needs AC help, feel free to send them our number."

Referral cards with the existing QR assets remain available; do not add incentives that
platform policies prohibit.

## 12. Lead handling and attribution

### Lead flow

Every lead, regardless of source, becomes a **lead record** before follow-up. Follow-up
speed target: **first response within business hours** (call/text back the same day; the
request form already acknowledges submissions).

### Lead tracking specification

Track these fields for every lead (a simple spreadsheet is sufficient; keep it private — no
customer data in the repository):

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Date | When the lead arrived |
| Time | Time of day (patterns by hour) |
| Customer | Name / identifying reference |
| ZIP | Service geography |
| City | Service geography |
| Service requested | Repair, airflow, maintenance, replacement, commercial, TAB |
| Lead source | Where the lead came from (channel) |
| Platform | Specific platform (GBP, Facebook, YouTube, …) |
| Campaign | UTM campaign where available |
| UTM | Full UTM string where available |
| Contact method | Phone / text / form |
| Booked | Yes/no |
| Appointment date | Scheduled date |
| Estimate amount | Where applicable |
| Estimate issued | Yes/no |
| Closed | Yes/no |
| Revenue | Where applicable |
| Reason lost | Honest reason when not closed |
| Notes | Anything operationally relevant |

**Attribution rules:** trust the UTM when present; ask new callers "how did you find us?"
when no UTM exists; never guess a source to make a channel look better. Do not put customer
names, addresses, or phone numbers into analytics tools — attribution lives in the private
lead sheet, not in GA4.

## 14. Commercial / TAB outbound system

Content alone is not enough for Engine B. Run explicit account-based outreach.

**Target accounts:** mechanical contractors · general contractors · commercial property
managers · facility managers · gyms / fitness facilities · retail centers · churches ·
restaurants · medical offices · professional offices · light-industrial facilities.

**Target activity:** build approximately **10–20 qualified accounts per week** once
commercial outbound begins. Quality over quantity. **No mass-spam campaigns.**

**Outreach structure:**

1. Who Sunshine Climate Solutions is
2. Specific service
3. Relevant technical proof
4. Geographic coverage (use the approved service area from `src/config/business.ts` —
   Hernando, Pasco, Pinellas and Hillsborough counties / Tampa Bay — never invent coverage)
5. Simple CTA

*Example style:*

> "Sunshine Climate Solutions provides HVAC field support and TAB/airflow verification for
> commercial projects. We handle measured airflow, RTU/AHU/VAV verification, deficiency
> documentation and closeout support. We serve Hernando, Pasco, Pinellas and Hillsborough
> counties. If you have an upcoming project requiring field measurement or balancing support,
> we can review the scope and project documents."

## 18. Decision rules

| Signal | Likely cause | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High views + no leads | Wrong audience, weak offer, weak CTA, or low buyer intent | Re-target the topic; sharpen the CTA; check whether the platform can even route buyers |
| Clicks + no calls | Landing-page / conversion issue | Review the owning page: CTA placement, clarity, mobile behavior |
| Calls + low booking | Phone handling, scheduling, qualification, or availability problem | Fix the response process before spending more on traffic |
| Bookings + low close rate | Sales, scope, offer, pricing, or expectation problem | Review estimate presentation and scope clarity |
| Strong close rate + low lead volume | Traffic / distribution shortage | Increase distribution of proven content; expand proven topics |
| Topic consistently generates qualified leads | Winning topic | Create more supporting content around it; never abandon a winner because it was "already covered" |

## 19. 30 / 60 / 90 day phases

**Days 1–30 — foundation.** Focus: repair, airflow, static pressure, electrical diagnosis,
maintenance. Operational goals: lead tracking active; review workflow active; GBP cadence
active; Field Proof habit established; owning pages strengthened; estimate follow-up working;
content workflow sustainable.

**Days 31–60 — expansion.** Focus: repair vs replace, ductwork + replacement, quote
comparison, commercial/TAB introduction. Operational goals: replacement buyer journey
established; email reactivation started; commercial prospecting started; LinkedIn cadence
established; Field Proof added to key website pages; retargeting considered only if tracking
works.

**Days 61–90 — optimization.** Focus: TAB, traverse, outside air, building pressure, humidity,
optimization. Operational goals: identify top 3 lead-generating topics; top 2 channels;
highest-value service category; strongest commercial response; cut low-value production
activity; increase investment in proven channels/topics.

## 20. Next 90-day rule

The next content plan must **not** automatically contain 12 unrelated new ideas. Use
approximately:

- **60–70%** expanded versions of topics/channels that generated actual qualified leads.
- **30–40%** new experiments.

Marketing compounds around evidence.

## 23. Fundraising distribution

The two owner-run campaigns (GoFundMe and GiveSendGo — URLs in
`src/config/business.ts` → `fundraising`) are a **separate support layer**, not a service
funnel. `/support/` is noindex and must never be mixed into service-conversion areas.

**Profile link vs fundraiser post link — keep them distinct:**

- **Business profile website fields** (Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, X, Gab, Parler, etc.)
  keep pointing to the **normal tracked SCS homepage URL**. Do not replace every social
  website field with `/support/`.
- **Fundraiser-specific posts** use the platform-specific tracked `/support/` link from the
  registry (list below).
- If a platform allows multiple profile links, the owner may add a secondary
  "Support SCS" link — but never replace the main business website.

**PRIMARY fundraising channels:**

- personal Facebook / direct personal network
- SCS Facebook
- Instagram
- YouTube founder video
- email / direct outreach

**SECONDARY:**

- TikTok
- X
- Gab
- Parler
- selective Nextdoor use

GoFundMe is the general-public/default campaign. GiveSendGo is the faith/community
alternative. Do not imply one platform is officially preferred over the other.

**Personal sharing vs business channels (intentional distinction):**

- Direct GoFundMe sharing from Aaron's **personal** profile/DMs can be appropriate when the
  goal is minimum friction for people who already know him.
- The **SCS business channels** should generally use the branded `/support/` page so visitors
  understand the story, see the founder investment, see both platforms, stay inside the SCS
  trust ecosystem, and can be attributed by channel.

**Keep customer acquisition active:** most SCS social output remains HVAC education, Field
Proof, reviews, service information and real field work. Suggested initial ratio:
approximately **15–25% fundraising content / 75–85% normal HVAC content** — a guide, not a
mechanical quota.

**Rules:**

- Do not make every SCS post a fundraiser post.
- Fundraiser updates should communicate progress, business-building milestones, or use of
  funds — never rescue framing, never fabricated progress, and never claims of tax
  deductibility, equity, repayment, or investment returns.
- Do not run cold paid traffic directly to the fundraiser without a separately approved
  campaign; paid customer-acquisition budget remains focused on generating HVAC customers.
- Do not spam all platforms with identical copy — adapt the framing per platform.
- Inbound links to `/support/` use the prepared registry links (`facebook_support_post`,
  `instagram_support_post`, `youtube_support`, `x_support_post`, `gab_support_post`,
  `parler_support_post`, `tiktok_support_video`, `nextdoor_support_post`, `email_support`).
  The external fundraiser URLs are outbound destinations and never enter the inbound UTM
  registry.
- The separate print QR `public/marketing/qr/support-campaign.svg` (registry link
  `print_support_qr`, `utm_source=print&utm_medium=qr&utm_campaign=support`) is for
  fundraiser/community handouts only — it does not replace the truck, business-card, service
  or review QRs.
- Outbound fundraiser clicks fire the fixed `scs_support_click` event
  (`support_platform`: `gofundme` | `givesendgo`) under the existing consent architecture;
  Umami records `support-gofundme-click` / `support-givesendgo-click`. This event measures
  outbound intent, not donations.

## 24. Guardrails (non-negotiable)

- No fabricated claims, measurements, reviews, job counts, availability, or savings.
- Field measurements require context; never present one measurement as a universal standard.
- Field Proof is never staged.
- No unsafe DIY electrical or refrigerant instructions.
- Do not attack competitors — teach customers how to evaluate scope.
- No fake urgency; no expiring offers unless they actually expire.
- Pricing, warranty wording, service area, and business facts come from
  `src/config/business.ts` — never invented in marketing copy.
